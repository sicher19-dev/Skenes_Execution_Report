What counts as executed

Every pitch is graded on one question: did it land in the spot that pitch is designed to reach in that count. Two count states are used, before two strikes and with two strikes. 3-2 is graded as a two-strike count. The strike zone is the batter's own zone, so heights are measured as a fraction of that hitter's zone.

The grade does not look at the swing, the call, the batted ball, or whether the pitch was the right call. Velocity and movement are not part of it either. Only where the ball crossed the plate.

Reading one start

A start is 60 to 110 pitches, so the executed share moves around on its own. The band is about 8 points either side of the season rate on a 95-pitch night, wider on shorter nights, and the first tab says whether the night cleared it. Single cells are smaller still: a 3-of-8 sinker line is a note, not a verdict. The season tab is where the pitch-by-pitch numbers settle. Miss distances use the median, so one spiked pitch does not move the typical miss.

The five grades

ExecutedThe pitch landed inside its target box.
Within one ballMissed the target by 3 inches or less, about one ball width. Big-league pitchers typically land 9 to 11 inches from the catcher's glove, so a miss this size is a good pitch by any standard.
CenteredLanded in the middle of the zone: the middle 8 inches of the plate, belt to mid-thigh. The costliest miss in any count.
Strike, off targetA strike, but not the designed spot and more than a ball away from it. For a two-strike pitch this is the one that came back into the zone.
Ball, off targetOutside the zone and more than a ball from the target. Before two strikes this is a wasted count; with two strikes it is a chase pitch that went nowhere.
